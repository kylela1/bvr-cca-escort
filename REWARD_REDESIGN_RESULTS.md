# Reward Redesign Results: Baseline vs. Mission-Aligned (Full-Scale, `new-code`)

This is the first real comparison for the project proposal: the existing (baseline) reward
vs. the mission-aligned redesign implemented this session, each trained for the full
10,000,000 timesteps (seed 42) that the thesis itself used. It follows the same format as
`PILOT_EXPERIMENT_RESULTS.md`, which covered the earlier 2,000,000-timestep pilot.

Figures: `figures/fig_c1_learning_curves_comparison.png`, `fig_c2_mission_outcomes_comparison.png`,
`fig_c3_reward_magnitude_comparison.png` (generated by `figures/generate_comparison_figures.py`).

---

## 1. What changed between the two runs

Both runs share every wrapper, hyperparameter, and training script except one flag:
`--reward-mode`. See §4 of `REWARD_SHAPING_STEPS_FORWARD.md` for the full rationale;
in short:

| | `reward_mode="baseline"` (`my_first_run`) | `reward_mode="mission_aligned"` (`reshaped_rewards1`) |
|---|---|---|
| Per-step tempo bonus after a kill | **+0.006/step** (up to ~+70/episode for loitering) | **0.0** (removed) |
| Per-step tempo penalty before a kill | -0.003/step (unchanged) | -0.003/step (unchanged) |
| HVAA destroyed | no direct penalty (only lost future tempo bonus) | **-15.0**, one-time |
| Mission success | no direct bonus (only via Godot's one-time `mission_accomplished_factor=+8`) | **+25.0** additional, one-time |
| Everything else (kill reward, action smoothness, heading-rate limit, NaN guard, obs, hyperparameters) | identical | identical |

**Caveat on this run's provenance**: `reshaped_rewards1`'s `training_results_seed42.json` was
not produced (the run's final-evaluation/save step did not complete cleanly — see §5), so
`reward_mode` is not literally recorded in a config file for this run. It is inferred from
strong numeric evidence: `best_checkpoint/best_model_info.json`'s individual episode rewards
cluster tightly at 46.6-54.7, matching the mission-aligned stack's theoretical terminal-reward
sum for a clean kill-and-complete episode (kill +12, Godot hit-enemy +10, mission success
+25, plus a small negative tempo term before the kill ≈ -3 to -4 → net ~44-47), which the
baseline stack cannot produce (its equivalent episodes score in the +70-100 range because of
the per-step loiter bonus). Treat this run's identity as very likely but not literally logged.

## 2. Full-scale results (20 evaluation episodes each, best checkpoint, seed 42)

| Metric | Baseline (`my_first_run`) | Mission-aligned (`reshaped_rewards1`) |
|---|---|---|
| Mean evaluation reward | **75.83 ± 27.96** | 47.46 ± 16.92 |
| Win rate (HVAA survives **and** CCA survives) | **95.0%** | 90.0% |
| HVAA survival rate | **95.0%** | 90.0% |
| Red kill rate | 95.0% | **100.0%** |
| Blue (CCA) loss rate | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| Mean episode length (steps) | 11,893.25 | 12,003.70 |
| Best checkpoint reached at timestep | 7,400,000 | 6,600,000 |

(Both are single-seed, 20-episode point estimates — see §6 before treating a 5-point win-rate
gap as significant.)

## 3. Findings

- **The reward magnitude drop is expected and not a regression.** Removing the +0.006/step
  post-kill tempo bonus removes roughly 70 points of reward from a typical ~12,000-step
  episode. `fig_c3` shows this directly: 75.8 → 47.5 mean reward, which is almost exactly
  that ~70-point difference net of the added +25/-15 terminal terms. **Compare mission-outcome
  rates, not raw reward, between these two runs** — that was the entire point of the redesign
  (Sec 1 of `REWARD_SHAPING_STEPS_FORWARD.md` showed raw reward rewarding loitering over
  finishing the mission).
- **Mission-level performance is essentially unchanged, on this single seed.** Win rate moved
  from 95.0% to 90.0% (1 episode out of 20) and red-kill rate moved from 95.0% to 100.0% (also
  1 episode). Both are within the noise you'd expect from a 20-episode sample of an already
  near-ceiling policy — this baseline was already winning 19/20 episodes, leaving very little
  room for the redesign to show a win-rate improvement even if the underlying policy is more
  robust. This is a meaningfully different situation from the pilot's baseline (`pilot_baseline`
  won ~0% at 2,000,000 timesteps) — at full scale, both reward designs converge to a strong
  policy, so win rate alone doesn't distinguish them here.
- **The training curves tell a different, more useful story than the final-checkpoint metrics
  do (`fig_c1`).** The mission-aligned run's evaluation reward rises alongside the baseline's
  through about 6,600,000 timesteps (where its best checkpoint was recorded), but then
  **declines steadily for the remaining ~3,400,000 timesteps** instead of continuing to
  improve. The baseline run keeps climbing through the full 10,000,000 timesteps. This is
  the single most important operational finding from this comparison: the sparser,
  mission-aligned reward is harder for PPO to keep climbing on once it reaches a good policy —
  plausibly because the dense per-step tempo signal was doing double duty as a stabilizing
  shaping signal for credit assignment, not just as a source of misalignment. Removing it
  bought better reward-mission alignment at the cost of training stability in the back half of
  the run.
  - Practical implication for your ablation: the rollback/checkpoint-selection mechanism
    (`RollbackEvaluationCallbackTopK`) already protects you from this — the *deployed* policy is
    still the 6,600,000-timestep checkpoint, not the degraded 10,000,000-timestep one. But if you
    run more mission-aligned configs, expect to see the same shape and don't be surprised by it;
    consider whether a shorter total-timestep budget (e.g. 7,000,000) would be more efficient for
    this reward mode specifically.
- **Both runs' LR-schedule pickling fix is now confirmed in production.** `reshaped_rewards1`
  had 5 rollback events (same as `my_first_run`) and its final `.zip` checkpoints are genuine
  6.7MB files, not the 0-byte files the bug produced before this session's fix in
  `rollback_eval_callback.py`.

## 4. What this does and doesn't establish yet

This is **one seed per condition**. It answers "does the redesigned reward measurably break
anything, and does it visibly change training dynamics?" — no, and yes, respectively. It does
**not** yet answer "is the mission-aligned reward reliably better/worse at win rate" — that
needs multiple seeds per condition (the thesis's own methodology uses 3+ seeds specifically
because single-seed win-rate differences of a few percentage points are not distinguishable
from training-seed noise). If the project proposal's ablation analysis is meant to make a
statistical claim about win rate, budget for at least 2-3 more seeds per reward mode before
reporting a win-rate delta as a finding.

## 5. Known gap in this run

`reshaped_rewards1`'s `training_results_seed42.json` (which records the exact CLI config,
including `reward_mode`) is missing from its run directory, even though checkpoints, monitor
logs, and TensorBoard events are all present and healthy — something after the final
checkpoint save but before the results-JSON write (train_bace_clean.py's final evaluation
pass or the `json.dump` at the very end of `main()`) did not complete. It did not affect
training or checkpoint quality, only this one summary file. If you rerun this comparison,
check for this file's presence as a quick "did the run finish cleanly" signal, and consider
wrapping that final block in a try/except (similar to the existing `model.save()` guard) if it
recurs.

## 6. Recommended next step

Given §3's finding, the highest-value next experiment is not "run mission_aligned again to
confirm win rate" but **either**: (a) add 2 more seeds to both `my_first_run` and
`reshaped_rewards1` to get a real 95% CI on the win-rate comparison, or (b) investigate the
mission-aligned run's post-6.6M decline directly (does it recover with a smaller LR after the
peak? does a longer `anneal_steps` on `ActionSmoothnessPenalty` help stabilize it?) since that
decline is the more scientifically interesting and more fixable result of the two.
